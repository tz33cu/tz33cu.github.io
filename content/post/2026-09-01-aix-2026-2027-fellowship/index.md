## About the Program

The aiX Faculty Fellowship Program is a [**faculty-led initiative**](https://provost.columbia.edu/content/faculty-led-initiatives) supported by the [Office of the Vice Provost for Faculty Advancement](https://provost.columbia.edu/content/office-vice-provost-faculty-advancement), in continued partnership with the [Center for Teaching and Learning](https://ctl.columbia.edu/). The program is led by aiX lab lead, **Professor Tian Zheng**.

aiX supports faculty **across disciplines** — arts, natural and social sciences, engineering, humanities, medicine and health, business, climate, journalism, law, social work, and beyond — in examining what AI means for teaching and learning in their field, and in building something concrete for their own students.

As a cross-disciplinary **eXchange**, aiX also creates space for fellows to share ideas, learn from one another, and collectively address the opportunities and challenges AI presents within and across disciplines.

### New Partnerships with Schools and Departments

New this year, aiX is developing **partnerships with individual schools and departments.** 

**With the Department of Statistics**, the program is integrated with the **"Statistical Consulting and Introduction to Data Science"** course. Student consultants in the course will learn how AI is reshaping different disciplines, study and discuss **AI education and research case studies**, and serve as consultants for fellowship projects that need **scoping and initial research**.

The aiX lab is also excited to share that **Professor Tian Zheng** and **Professor Rebecca Wexler** (an aiX fellow from Spring 2026) have received an [**Interdisciplinary Teaching Initiative**](https://vptli.columbia.edu/interdisciplinary-teaching/) grant from the [Provost's Teaching and Learning Grants Program](https://vptli.columbia.edu/grants/) to develop an **AI literacy module for all first-year law students**.

## This Year's Focus: Teaching *with* AI, Teaching *about* AI

The 2026–2027 fellowship is more sharply focused than previous cycles. Fellows will explore two intertwined questions in the context of their discipline:

- **Teaching with AI** — how AI tools change what and how we teach, what students practice, and how we assess learning.
- **Teaching about AI** — treating AI as an object of disciplinary study: how these systems work, what they are made of, and what they do to knowledge, learning, and professional practice in the field.

## What Fellows Will Do

- Participate in a **kick-off bootcamp** on **AI literacy** and **AI education design**.
- Design and develop a **discipline-specific AI education project** — a course module, an assignment sequence, a curricular revision, a learning tool, or another concrete artifact for their students — advancing it through the project lifecycle (see [Build Readiness](#build-readiness)).
- Work **closely with student consultants and designers** throughout the fellowship.
- Join **Zoom consultation hours** and/or **walk-in studio hours** for ongoing design support.
- Contribute to a cross-disciplinary **community of practice** alongside projects at every stage of development.
- Present outcomes at a **public share event** at the end of the year.

## Build Readiness: Matching Support to Project Stage {#build-readiness}

Applications will be **triaged by "build readiness"**: how far along the project idea is. Every project sits somewhere on a lifecycle:

**Ideation → Scoping → Research & Design → Development → Evaluation**

A promising idea that is still forming needs brainstorming, consultation, and framing; a project ready to be built needs tech talent, compute, and development. aiX will support projects **differently depending on where they are**, so that resources go where they can actually be used.

| Project stage | What aiX provides |
|---|---|
| **Ideation** | Kick-off bootcamp; work with consultants to sharpen the question, survey what exists, and develop the idea further. |
| **Scoping** | Bootcamp and consultation to define scope, learning goals, audience, and success criteria — the step that turns an idea into a buildable project. |
| **Research & Design** | **Student intern support** and **AI credits**, plus design consultation and studio hours. |
| **Development** | **Student intern support** and **AI credits**; hands-on building with the Design Studio. |
| **Evaluation** | Support for assessing the project with students, and preparation for the public share event. |

{{% callout note %}}
**Intern support and AI credits go to projects at the Research & Design stage and beyond.** Fellows whose projects are still at Ideation or Scoping will start by participating in the bootcamp and working with consultants to develop their ideas. Once an idea becomes concrete, the project **moves into Research & Design and becomes eligible for intern support** during the fellowship year.
{{% /callout %}}

### What Fellows Receive

**All fellows:**

- Participation in the **kick-off bootcamp** on AI literacy and AI education design.
- **Zoom consultation hours** and **walk-in studio hours**.
- Membership in the aiX **community of practice** and the year-end public share event.

**Fellows at Research & Design and beyond:**

- **Support from student consultants and designers**, guided by senior aiX Design Studio interns (see [The Design Studio](#the-design-studio) below).
- **Up to $1,000 in AI credits** for developing and testing their project.

## A Community of Practice: "Everyone's Backyard" {#community-of-practice}

Because projects enter at different stages, the fellowship cohort will always include work at **every point in the lifecycle** — some fellows still framing a question, others deep in development, others evaluating with students. 

This is the **"Everyone's Backyard"** model of the [aiX lab]({{< relref "/post/2025-12-29-conv-design-studio/" >}}) where we learn from each other and contribute ideas to each other's projects — across disciplines and across stages.

## The Design Studio {#the-design-studio}

This year the aiX program will leverage **"Statistical Consulting and Introduction to Data Science,"** a course taught by Professor Tian Zheng ([course repository](https://github.com/TZstats-Columbia/ColabDataScience-DesignStudio)). Students enrolled in the course will serve as **consultants and designers** on fellows' projects, working with fellows as part of their coursework — with particular strength in supporting projects at the **scoping and initial research** stages.

In addition, a small number of **senior design studio interns** with advanced **agentic AI skills** will be recruited to support project success and to guide the course-based student teams.

{{% callout note %}}
**There will not be an open call for design studio interns this year.** Students interested in this work should enroll in the course; senior intern positions will be filled by direct recruitment.
{{% /callout %}}
